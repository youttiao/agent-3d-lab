# AI 3D Geometry for Mechanical / Dimensioned / Functional Parts — The Quality Gap
**Landscape report, evidence as of 2026-11-23.** All primary sources fetched directly (arXiv HTML/PDF, GitHub API, vendor docs, benchmark leaderboards). Where a number exists only in a Chinese-language aggregator blog or a vendor marketing page, it is flagged as such.

---

## 0. Headline

**The gap is not "the model is not smart enough." It is that the dominant 2026 architectures produce the wrong *kind* of artifact for mechanical work, and the benchmarks that would expose that have only just started to exist.**

Three findings carry the report:

1. **The only dimensional measurement of the flagship text-to-3D meshes is catastrophic.** On 251,000 dimensioned engineering drawings (OmniMech, arXiv 2608.05539), **TRELLIS's volume error is 317.9%**, Hunyuan3D-2's 109.4%, InstantMesh's 5,515.5%. Not one of the six named text-to-mesh products ships STEP, B-rep, or a tolerance.
2. **"It runs" and "it's right" are now separately measured, and the gap is large.** On build123d/OpenCASCADE, LLM CAD agents claimed completion on 98% of attempts and were **silently wrong on 14.1%** (arXiv 2611.10063). The paper's framing sentence is the single best quotable line in the whole corpus: *"The expensive failure is a part that builds and renders plausibly while its bolt circle sits on the wrong pitch diameter, delivered with 'done, confidence 0.97.'"*
3. **The best 2026 results come from *not* letting the model write the numbers.** CADMAGEDDON's architectural bet is *"a vision–language model chooses only the structure of each feature, and fitting supplies its values"* (arXiv 2611.22563). It beats frontier models on surface fidelity while running on one 48 GB GPU. mesh2cad, which is not learned at all, converts 6/6 real Prusa printer parts to **exact STEP** with Hausdorff ≤0.22 mm and volume error ≤0.39%.

**Exact failure taxonomy** — quoted from §4.2 of the paper:

- **Structural gates:** *"Structural gates also require one non-empty `PartDesign::Body` producing one solid through allowed native features rather than baked shape holders. Candidates with more than 5,000 faces, or face/vertex counts differing from the reference by more than 50% of the larger count, are rejected. Gate failures override the spatial multiplier's nonzero floor."*
- **Bounding-box gate:** *"An independently measured OCCT oriented bounding box is a gate, not an additional weighted subscore: maximum relative error across sorted extents of 10% or more sets g = 0."*
- **Spec-parameter mismatch at 1% tolerance:** *"The default scalar relative tolerance is 1%, with case-specific measurement logic and binding tolerances where applicable."* Score: `s = max(0, 1 − f/min(n,10))` — for n ≥ 10, *"each failed or unmeasurable parameter costs 0.1."*
- **Feature-history mismatch:** *"Face-center alignment depends on face decomposition, so congruent geometry built through a different feature history need not receive g = 1."*
- **Volume credit is brutally tight:** *"Volume receives full credit at relative error at most 0.1% and zero subscore at 1% or more."*

**Fraction of trials scoring zero — the single most useful number in the paper:**

> *"Across the 1,300 selected final trials from the 13 evaluated systems, **393 (30.23%) earn zero reward**."*

By workflow: **creation 22.56%, create-and-edit 41.54%, image-to-CAD 27.50%.** And: *"Five tasks receive zero from every system, all in create-and-edit."* Of the 393 zeros, *"326 involve a zero geometry component, including 50 that also have a zero specification component. Another 33 fail only specification consistency; the remainder comprise 23 final exceptions, 7 missing outputs, and 4 scorer crashes."*

**Why editing is much worse than creation — the paper's data:**

| | Zero-outcome rate | Best point estimate | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Creation | 22.56% | 55.48% (Fable 5.1 + Claude Code) | base reference models average 5.20 features |
| Create-and-edit | **41.54%** | 44.37% (Astra + Codex) | base references average only 2.47 features; targets 5.60 |

Two structural reasons, both quoted:

1. **The scoring nests four gates instead of two.** `r_edit = H(H(g_base, s_base), H(g_target, s_target))` where `H(g,s) = 0 if g = 0 or s = 0`. The paper's own gloss: *"Thus a zero score on a required axis or deliverable cannot be rescued by another axis."* A create-and-edit task therefore has **four** independent zero-paths instead of two. *(Inference, not a paper claim: the paper does not decompose this, but the aggregation mechanically penalizes the two-stage workflow. If each gate passed independently with probability p, create-nonzero ≈ p² and edit-nonzero ≈ p⁴; the observed 0.774 vs 0.585 is consistent with p ≈ 0.87, i.e. most of the 41.54% vs 22.56% gap is consistent with gate count rather than intrinsic editing difficulty.)*
2. **The edit is genuinely harder construction.** The exemplar task `freecad-ff7883ab0c` requires the agent to build an axisymmetric flanged inlet housing, then add *"a fourfold-symmetric front plate, 16.51 mm thick, whose outline joins side, corner, and transition arcs with short tangent connectors… four 13.34 mm through bolt holes… and four 19.05 mm openings cut 34.54 mm deep from the rear flange face, each pattern placed from a signed seed center at uniform angular spacing."* 15 base parameters + 14 target parameters = 29 gates, any one of which zeroes the task.

**The paper's own caveat, verbatim from the abstract:**

> *"These measurements describe complete systems, not isolated model capabilities."*

And from §6: *"Grader rewards are heuristic proxies: geometric similarity and checked parameters do not certify all future edits, design intent, or engineering validity."* Also: *"Create-and-edit measures two-stage construction, not maintenance of an externally authored document."*

**Worked example of what a failure looks like** (trussed plate, `freecad-…`): 9 of 13 systems score zero. *"Eight of these zeros come from the specification axis alone. These outcomes have geometry similarity between 0.126 and 0.445, but 12 to 80 of the 86 parameters are inconsistent or not found, which exhausts the failure budget of 10."* So: **the shape was roughly right and the dimensions were not** — exactly the mechanical-part failure mode.

And the clevis connector, the opposite pattern: *"Two of them… pass every parameter check yet earn 0.419 because their geometry similarity is 0.265."*

**Also from this paper — the critical distinction for a mechanical engineer:** *"Our benchmark instead requires native FreeCAD documents with an application-managed feature tree and document dependencies, rather than a CadQuery program or exported B-rep alone."* And: *"These checks are proxies for the intended engineering requirements; they do not by themselves certify manufacturing readiness or functional editability under arbitrary future modifications."*

### 2.2 RealCADBench — arXiv 2609.03773 ✓ VERIFIED

> *"Across the six frontier-scale large models, **executability ranges from 0.565 to 0.812, Solid IoU from 0.2841 to 0.5379, and Surface IoU from 0.112 to 0.217** across the four Part regimes."*

**What these numbers mean for "does the produced CAD actually match the request":**

- **Executability 0.565–0.812** — the FreeCAD Python script runs and exports a solid 56.5–81.2% of the time. Best case: ~1 in 5 attempts produces nothing.
- **Solid IoU 0.2841–0.5379** — the *volume* overlap with the reference. Even the best system is under 54% on volumetric agreement. An L-bracket with 4 holes whose holes are in the wrong place will still show IoU ≈ 0.9 because the holes are a small fraction of the volume — which is why the paper insists on a second metric.
- **Surface IoU 0.112–0.217** — the *surface* overlap. **This is the number that matters for mechanical parts, and it is roughly half of Solid IoU and one-quarter of what a good reconstruction achieves.** A part can occupy the right volume while every functional surface is wrong.
- **Judge 0–100** — rubric-based visual-semantic identity. *"no model leads all four metrics."*

Recurring failure modes, quoted: *"most notably **missing fine structures, loss of part identity, and incorrect assembly placement**."* And from §4.3: *"A syntactically valid program may still omit mounting holes from a bracket. A plausible render may still place the wrong components in an assembly."*

Assembly data: GPT-5.4 and Codex+GPT-5.5 **execute successfully on all 25 assembly tasks** yet score Surface IoU **0.1046** and **0.1161**. *"Because the two studies are unmatched, the 0.1781 difference reflects two different populations rather than a paired comparison."* Codex+GPT-5.5 vs standalone GPT-5.5: executability +0.1600, Solid IoU +0.0714, Surface IoU +0.0190, **but Judge −6.98 pp** — *"An agent loop can fix syntax errors, execution failures, and some construction mistakes without necessarily correcting the core design decision."*

### 2.5 "Done Is Not Correct" — arXiv 2611.10063 ✓ VERIFIED INDEPENDENTLY

The most directly on-point paper for "the LLM writes code that runs but is wrong."

Built on **build123d/OpenCascade**. 13 tasks, 122 machine-checkable checks, 4 models, n=156 attempts per architecture. Tasks are *"T1 single feature (flange with bolt circle…)"*, T4 = *"edit-under-constraint (modify provided code: re-pattern a bolt circle; enlarge a bore…)"*.

> *"single-shot agents claimed completion on **98% of attempts and were silently wrong on 14.1%** (task-cluster CI [5.8, 24.4]); a tool-use loop that enforces **measure-then-claim** cut silent failures to **3.2%** (McNemar p < 10⁻⁴) and raised **claim precision from 64% to 96%**."*

> *"Crashes are cheap to catch: a spec that fails to execute announces itself. **The expensive failure is a part that builds and renders plausibly while its bolt circle sits on the wrong pitch diameter, delivered with 'done, confidence 0.97.'**"*

> *"of 151 failed attempts, **95 never produced a valid solid**… and **56 produced valid geometry that failed semantic checks**. Among the latter only, the dominant signatures are **missing or mis-sized internal features** (counterbore depth or face, bore and hole extents, step sections); **a wrong envelope dimension is also common**."*

Their own oracle is self-audited and admits leniency: *"Hardening our own oracle flipped 18 of 519 regraded verdicts from pass to fail and none in reverse."* And their pre-registered prediction P3 **failed**: *"the silent-failure rate improves much less with model capability than syntactic success does."*

**Crucially: the fix is a contract, not a bigger model.** *"the reduction to 4–5% comes from the claim contract, not from capability."*

**The paper's own limitations are the load-bearing part for mechanical work (§F, verbatim):**

> *"First, similar to other voxel-based methods, **O-Voxel's representation power is bounded by its spatial resolution**. For detailed geometric features smaller than the voxel size, the Flexible Dual Grid formulation could produce aliasing artifacts. For example, when two parallel surfaces that are very close to each other intersect the same voxel, the QEF solver, by design, will place the dual vertex at a position that minimizes the error to both surfaces, **often resulting in a vertex located between them rather than accurately on one**."*

> *"Second, we observe that the reconstructed and generated results sometimes contain **small holes**, though they can mostly be rectified with standard mesh post-processing techniques (e.g., hole filling). We attribute this issue to challenges in the sparse nature of our decoder, where **ensuring a perfectly closed, manifold surface** from the high-resolution sparse structure predicted by our decoder can be difficult."*

> *"Finally, our O-Voxel is currently focused on geometry and material and **it does not explicitly encode higher-level structural or semantic information**."*

**And the representation is *designed* to be non-manifold.** From §3.1.1: *"it is free from the watertight and manifold constraints, enabling robust handling of arbitrary geometry including **self-intersecting surfaces** and fully-enclosed interior structures."* The official README lists *"✅ Open Surfaces, ✅ Non-manifold Geometry"* as features. The official HF card (`microsoft/TRELLIS.2-4B`) states under "Known Limitations": *"the generated raw meshes may occasionally contain small holes or minor topological discontinuities. **For applications requiring strictly watertight geometry (e.g., 3D printing), we provide accompanying mesh post-processing scripts, such as hole-filling algorithms.**"*

**Keyword census over the full TRELLIS.2 paper text (24 pp.):** `watertight` → **0**; `printab*` → **0**; `toleran*` → **0**; `thin wall` → **0**; `CAD` → **0**; `STEP` → **0**; `fillet`/`chamfer` (other than the Chamfer-Distance metric)/`GD&T`/`clearance`/`through-hole` → **0**.

**Four caveats you must carry with this number:**
1. **Vendor self-report.** Hi3D measured itself and its competitors. TRELLIS.2 and Pixal3D publish **no** printability claim — the 17% and 7% figures have exactly one source.
2. **The test-set size N is never stated.** The report says only *"the fraction of all test assets."* **COULD NOT VERIFY.**
3. **Not reproducible.** Hi3D's own README: *"Training code and the paper's evaluation harness are not included."*
4. **SSR ≠ manufacturability — Hi3D says so itself:** *"Watertightness and successful slicing are useful indicators for fabrication-oriented workflows, but they **do not guarantee physical manufacturability**."*

**The most parsimonious reading of Hi3D's own table contradicts its own framing:** SSR *rises* at low resolution (97–100% at 256–512³) and *collapses* at high resolution (80% at 1024³, 17%/7% at 1536³). The higher the model's geometric resolving power, the less printable its output — because resolving sub-voxel thin features produces geometry a 0.4 mm nozzle cannot slice. Also note the comparison set contains **TripoSG at 100%** and **Hunyuan3D-2.1 at 97%**, both *lower* resolution than Hi3D.

**Hi3D's other mechanical-relevant admissions (README + docs, verified):**
- *"Output is recentered and normalized to unit maximum radius, so **input world coordinates and absolute scale are not preserved**."*
- *"Coarse structure matters. **Severe structural errors in the coarse mesh can persist.** Geometry hidden from the image is a plausible completion."*
- Postprocessing: *"pyfqmr simplifies toward 1,000,000 faces at 512³ or 1024³, and 2,000,000 at 1536³."* — **against Parametric CAD Bench v3's hard gate of 5,000 faces.** Two orders of magnitude apart in what each representation considers a "part."
- *"Seeds are not mesh hashes… GPU sparse convolution and attention are not bitwise deterministic."*
- *"Geometric detail and nominal resolution **do not guarantee fitness for a downstream application**."*

## 6. The mesh→CAD repair round trip — does it work in practice?

**Why you would do it at all.** From CADENA (2608.00799), verbatim:
> *"**A mesh can be rendered and printed but not changed: it has no notion of a bore, a fillet or a wall, only triangles, so there is no handle by which to widen a hole or lengthen a bracket.** A part is almost never reused exactly as found — it must be adapted to the standards of the product it goes into — and every such adaptation is an edit to a parameter that only a program exposes. The case is stronger still for scanned input, whose surfaces are noisy and edges rounded: **fitting geometry to such a measurement reproduces its defects, whereas recovering a program reconstructs what the part is** — a cylinder of some diameter, a hole at some position — and with it idealised, manufacturable geometry."*

### 6.1 mesh2cad — the strongest positive evidence ✓ VERIFIED (GitHub API + docs)

github.com/emirmrkaljevic/mesh2cad, 137★, MIT. *"converts an STL mesh of a simple mechanical part into editable parametric CAD: STEP, CadQuery, OpenSCAD, and FreeCAD scripts. It is also an MCP server."*

**Scope — exactly the classes you asked about, verbatim:**
> *"It is meant for simple mechanical parts: **plates, brackets, and boxes with holes, bolt circles, slots, bosses (including on an extruded outline), countersunk and counterbored holes on that outline, and fillets.** A Z extrusion can also carry one radius on every vertical corner of the outline. It fits planes, cylinders, spheres, cones, and constant-radius rounds."*

**The honest failure list** (docs/limits.md, docs/real-parts.md), verbatim:
- **Fillets degrade first.** *"At 0.2 mm [noise] the bracket falls back on every seed… one seed keeps only three of the four rounds and the Hausdorff rises to 0.85 mm."*
- **Countersinks are noise-sensitive.** *"Countersinks and narrow chamfers are exact through about 0.05 mm of noise… at 0.2 mm noise is exact on one of three seeds. On the other two the conical band and the hole wall meet at a single vertex, so those seeds fall back to a valid faceted solid."*
- **Split circular features get sewn.** *"One of those circles is split across four coplanar pads that do not touch, and is sewn as four arcs."*
- **Boolean silhouettes can blow up.** *"The intersection of the full-length silhouettes is 7.924 mm off (30.07% volume), over the limit of 0.266 mm, so the script is a union of partial-depth extrusions instead."*
- *"It does not recover organic or sculpted shapes, scans, or free-form surfaces. **When a fit is poor it does not invent a surface.**"*
- **Refusal behaviour:** *"If the part is outside what a script can express, or the rebuilt solid misses the check, the script is not written and the command exits with code 2."*

**The four CADENA failure modes (Appendix A.7, verbatim) — this is the cleanest statement of why mesh→CAD still fails on real parts:**

> *"**Five parts on which CADENA fails**… They illustrate three of the four modes: **countable features are approximated rather than counted**, so the impeller and the radial fan keep the right body and the wrong blades; **helical geometry outside the DSL's vocabulary is replaced by the nearest available primitive**, a thread standing in for a coil spring; and **where no single canonical view resolves the shape, the reconstruction collapses to a rough envelope of it, as in the valve body and the bracket.** **None of these is a failure to emit valid CadQuery — every reconstruction shown builds and is watertight, and would be counted in the means of Table 1.**"
>
> 1. *"Countable features are approximated rather than counted… the residual after the body is built is a thin, repetitive shell that changes little whether the count is right or off by one, so the signal that would correct the count is weak exactly where it is needed."*
> 2. *"Occluded geometry is invisible to the observation… Internal cavities, blind bores and undercuts that project identically to a solid region are not represented in the observation at all, so **no amount of stepping recovers them**."*
> 3. *"Out-of-vocabulary features are approximated by what the DSL has… The result is often close in Chamfer distance and **clearly wrong to an engineer**, which is precisely the discrepancy GMS is designed to expose."*
> 4. *"Early frame errors are not recovered."*

**CADFather's cost analysis (the killer comparison for approach (a) at scale), verbatim:**
> *"CADFather reaches an IoU of 0.943 after four assistant steps at **$0.016 per part**, and 0.968 at its full budget of **$0.046 per part**. For comparison, **Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra with tools report 0.962 at $5.94 per part and 0.959 at $1.75 per part**, respectively."*

**Verdict for the round trip:** it **demonstrably works** for exactly the classes you named — plates, brackets, boxes with holes, bolt circles, countersunk/counterbored holes, simple fillets — *when the source mesh came from a CAD tool* (long, clean triangles) rather than a noisy scan. It fails on fillets/countersinks above ~0.05–0.2 mm noise, on spheres, on freeform/ribbed outlines, and on features split across coplanar pads. The **deterministic** path (mesh2cad) is dramatically more reliable on real mechanical parts than any *learned* path. And even the best learned system (CADMAGEDDON) still cannot deliver named parameters: *"its dimensions are literal values rather than named parameters, and fillets and chamfers are explicit solids rather than edge operations; **recovering design intent is beyond the current system**."*

## 7. The self-hosted "AI CAD harness" reality in 2026

### 7.1 Kernel-level failure modes from the issue trackers ✓ VERIFIED

Repo state (2026-11-23): **build123d** `gumyr/build123d` — 3,822★, 226 open issues; **CadQuery** `CadQuery/cadquery` — 6,153★, 487 open issues.

**Fillet/chamfer failures are the single most-cited failure mode:**

- **build123d #1506** — *"When `Solid.fillet` fails, the only message is `Failed creating a fillet with radius of 3, try a smaller value or use max_fillet() to find the largest valid fillet radius` which doesn't say which of the selected edges is the problem. For a selection of a dozen edges the user is left bisecting by hand, **and this is the most common thing people get stuck on.**"*
- **CadQuery #2114** — *"`cadquery.func.fillet` and `cadquery.func.chamfer` call `builder.Build()` and then `builder.Shape()`. If the build failed, `BRepBuilderAPI_MakeShape::Shape()` calls `Build()` again, and the second build of `BRepFilletAPI_MakeFillet` / `MakeChamfer` **segfaults inside OCCT**. **Headline: there is no 2026 arXiv paper in which an agent uses an actual 3D printer or robot arm as the primary feedback loop to verify and repair the CAD of a mechanical part.** Four tiers exist:

| 2. Rigid-body simulation of a mechanism | Chrono + Bullet simulation of candidate-specific physical tests | **FunCAD-Harness / FunCADBench** (arXiv 2610.26861) |
| 3. Slicer / DFM rules | 45° overhang detection, DFM rules, G-code | **AgentsCAD** (arXiv 2607.02448) |
| 4. Render + kernel only | Renders + OpenCASCADE measurements | CADSmith (2603.26512), Adam, Zoo Zookeeper |

**RoboBuilder (2611.23855)** is the strongest real-physical evidence:
> *"coding agents co-design a robot's hardware and program from standard parts, motors and a **custom 3D-printed part**, and every design that passes in simulation is built and tested on a physical course. **Simulation alone is not enough: under public part data, none of 15 initial passing designs crossed the real course**, so an agent learns the simulator from measurements it requests and videos of real runs."*

Scale: **135 physical robots built by hand from ~13,000 pieces, ≈240 hours**; 16,427 simulations of 12,774 distinct robots; 461 paired real videos released. The custom part is *"written by the agent in CadQuery"* and 3D-printed.
> *"Of the designs that pass in the learned simulator, **66% cross the real course: 77% at level 1, 68% at level 2 and 42% at level 3**."*
> *"One round of the loop removes 72% of these failures (from 18 to 5), and the share of simulated passes that cross for real rises from 73% to 93%."*
> *"The printed part **rescues nine failed tasks**, mostly at level 3."*

**Framing caveat:** RoboBuilder is a *robot* benchmark, not a mechanical-part benchmark; its CAD artifact is one bespoke part per robot.

**FunCAD-Harness (2610.26861)** — *"Can It Actually Work? Synthesizing Functional CAD Mechanisms"* — FunCADBench, **46 mechanism families** (clamps, transmissions, counters, alarm clocks, coin sorters). Simulation: *"**Project Chrono** for rigid-body dynamics and **Bullet** for collision detection."*

| Setting | Astra | Grok |
|---|---|---|
| Requirements only (agent picks own verification) | **1/46** | 3/46 |
| Simulation tools | 8/46 | 10/46 |
| Binary pass/fail feedback (no diagnostics) | **0/46** | 8/46 |
| Full harness, **constructor vision removed** | 20/46 | 18/46 |
| **Full harness** | **22/46** | 19/46 |

Two findings matter enormously for the "physical feedback" argument: removing **diagnostic** feedback collapses Astra from 22 tasks to **zero**, while removing *vision* cost only 2 tasks. *"Agents obtain geometric and physical information primarily through CAD programs and simulation, with little use of rendered images."* And: *"Because fabricating and assembling each candidate is costly, physical simulation offers a practical way to examine these interactions."*

- **Tripo and Meshy ship no first-party CAD/STEP converter.** Their tool catalogues (Tripo: 3D File Converter, STL Repair, STL Editor, Mesh Compressor, Mesh Simplifier, Normal Map Generator, 3D Model Splitter; Meshy: Analyze/Repair Printability, Remesh, Resize, UV Unwrap, Auto Split, Relief, Balance) contain **no CAD/STEP path**.

## 10. Concrete scenarios — four real parts × four approaches

### Part 1: **L-bracket, 4 × Ø6.0 through-holes on a 40 × 60 mm bolt pattern, 8 mm thick**

| Approach | What happens | Why | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(a) Code-first** | Best case. `Box` → `Hole` ×4 → export STEP, **if** the agent picks the right workplane, uses `Hole` not `cut(Cylinder)`, and puts the pattern at exactly (10,10)/(30,10)/(30,50)/(10,50). **Failure mode: the hole exists but is Ø8** (spec mismatch), or the boolean silently produces no hole, or `cutThruAll` recall is 16.2%. If Ø6.0 H7 with tolerance — **the tolerance almost certainly does not survive**: the tolerance lives in the spec, not in the script. | Parametric CAD Bench v3: 8/9 zero-reward attempts on a 86-parameter task failed on the **specification axis alone** with geometry similarity 0.126–0.445. BuildCAD: `hole` recall 60.2%, `cutThruAll` 16.2%, `cboreHole` 0.0%. Build123d #1363: boolean subtraction silently produces no hole in the exported STEP. BenchCAD F01: *"Model writes radius 5.6 when prompt asks for 5.85."* | 2611.02659, 2605.10865, build123d#1363 |
| **(b) Text-to-mesh** | **Fails outright.** You get a mesh of "an L-bracket" with no holes, or holes as non-circular blobs. No diameter, no position, no through-ness, no tolerance. Absolute scale is destroyed (Hi3D normalizes to unit radius; TRELLIS.2 exports a unit cube). You cannot cut the hole to Ø6 because there is no cylinder to cut. | TRELLIS.2 §3.1.1: O-Voxel is *"free from the watertight and manifold constraints"*; holes are not in its vocabulary (0 hits for `hole` as a feature). OmniMech: TRELLIS volume error 317.9%. Hi3D: *"input world coordinates and absolute scale are not preserved."* | 2512.14692, 2608.05539, Hi3D docs |
| **(c) GUI-driven** | The agent sketches a rectangle on a plane, pads it, then attempts 4 × Hole via the Hole tool. Reaches at best **22.1% on Part tasks**. Most likely failure: wrong workplane for the second hole, or the Hole dialog's depth default leaving it blind, or **F6 (close but out of tolerance)**. The 50-task terminal-only ablation shows the modal failure is *"wrong document structure."* | CADWorld: 17.5% best overall, 22.1% on Part, 0% on CAM/FEM/TechDraw; F6/F8 defined explicitly. | 2609.16251 |
| **(d) Bridge** | **The strongest path.** text-to-cad (build123d/OCCT) writes a real parametric script with named `hole_d`, and ships STEP/STL/3MF; a DFM check and an OrcaSlicer slice are built in. If you started from a mesh, mesh2cad's *bolt circle* primitive fitting handles this class directly. But: **no tolerance/GD&T**, and the DFM checker explicitly says *"A mesh cannot be checked for holes, pins or fine detail."* | text-to-cad README; mesh2cad README; Zoo KCL known-issues (*"coplanar booleans not currently supported"* — coplanar is exactly a bolt circle on a flat plate). | GitHub, zoo.dev |

**Verdict:** (d) > (a) > (c) ≫ (b). The Ø6 H7 *tolerance* is not delivered by any of them — at best you get Ø6.000 geometrically with no fit class attached.

### Part 2: **Robot finger link — 42 mm proximal phalanx, 18 mm wide, pivot holes at exact 38.0 mm centers**

| Approach | What happens | Why | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(a) Code-first** | This is where parametric wins decisively: the link becomes a **named parameter set** (`pivot_center_distance = 38.0`), so changing it re-drives the geometry. The failure is exactly the parametric-CAD-Bench create-and-edit regime: *"5 tasks receive zero from every system, all in create-and-edit"*, and the edit exemplar requires *"each pattern placed from a signed seed center at uniform angular spacing"* — i.e. exactly a pivot pattern. **Expect 39.69–44.37%, not 61%.** | Parametric CAD Bench v3: create-and-edit 41.54% zero-outcome rate vs creation 22.56%. | 2611.02659 |
| **(b) Text-to-mesh** | **Catastrophic and specifically so for a finger.** TRELLIS.2's 95th-percentile dihedral is **73.11°** — a pivot hole wall should be 90°. The holes will be smooth dimples. Link length is a *ratio*, so "42 mm" is lost with everything else. Realistic use: use it as a **visual reference** for a human, not as the part. | Procedura MechBench-36: *"a surface regressed from samples has no notion of a face or a bore."* | 2608.26238 |
| **(c) GUI-driven** | A finger link is a sketch with 2 arcs + 2 holes + a fillet + a pattern — 4-6 GUI interactions, each state-dependent. CADWorld's median human reference is 26.5 actions; agents average 71.8–104.6 actions on failures. The fillet and the hole-edge blend are the classic failure points. | CADWorld Table 3; F6/F8. | 2609.16251 |
| **(d) Bridge** | Best mechanism if you want a **kinematically valid** part: Procedura *"writes the object as a procedural assembly, a parametric program whose named parts are joined by typed, machine-checkable mates… solving each placement from the mated frames rather than guessing it"*. FunCAD-Harness's rigid-body simulation then verifies the linkage actually moves. But CADMAGEDDON's honest limit applies: *"fillets and chamfers are explicit solids rather than edge operations."* | Procedura abstract; FunCAD-Harness 22/46 with full harness; CADMAGEDDON limitations. | 2608.26238, 2610.26861, 2611.22563 |

**Verdict:** (d) for the assembly + kinematics, (a) for the parametric reusability. (b) is only usable as a silhouette.

### Part 3: **Camera mount — 1/4-20 UNC threaded boss, 6.35 mm pilot, on an extruded rail with a T-slot**

| Approach | What happens | Why | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(a) Code-first** | A threaded boss is a **thread feature**. Threads are in the DSL but the LLM must get the *pitch and handedness and depth* right, and the **thread profile** is exactly what CAD-bench tests. BenchCAD's per-op recall table has no `thread` row, but CAD-bench (functional) reports *"functional tasks remain near zero for most standalone runs."* Realistically: the model produces a smooth Ø6.35 hole labeled a thread, or a helix, or a chamfered hole. | CAD-bench abstract (17 tasks, four tiers, "threaded mating pairs", thread-profile analysis); Text2CAD-Bench L3 collapse (*"advanced features such as sweep, loft, and shell operations cause execution rates to plummet"*). | icml.cc/virtual/2026/67851, 2605.18430 |
| **(b) Text-to-mesh** | **Worst case of the four parts.** Threads are the canonical case where perceptual fidelity and functional fidelity diverge maximally: a helix renders convincingly and screws in as nothing. TRELLIS.2's paper shows it handles *"internal enclosed structures"* — but a thread is a *helical surface*, and CADENA's failure mode list says it directly: *"helical geometry outside the DSL's vocabulary is replaced by the nearest available primitive, **a thread standing in for a coil spring**."* A mesh thread has no pitch parameter to change. | CADENA A.7; TRELLIS.2 limitations; CAD-bench's thread-profile check existing at all is the evidence that this is a known failure. | 2608.00799, 2512.14692 |
| **(c) GUI-driven** | A threaded boss in a GUI CAD means a Thread feature with a standard library. This is one of the few mechanical tasks where a GUI is *advantageous*, because the thread standard is a menu pick rather than a parameter the model must invent. But CADWorld's Assembly tasks (25) require "correct assembly placement" and the best agent gets ~0% on specialized workflows. | CADWorld taxonomy; Zoo KCL known-issues (KCL lacks declarative part positioning). | 2609.16251, zoo.dev |
| **(d) Bridge** | The correct answer, because the thread standard is a **library lookup, not a generation**. Zoo's KCL Standard Library has *holes*; BuildCAD ships a *thread generator*; text-to-cad ships engineering drawings with *"hole callouts"*. Zoo's Zookeeper is *"directly integrated into the Zoo CAD engine to generate, modify and validate 3D geometries"* — validation is the point. | zoo.dev/docs/kcl; buildcad.ai; texttocad.dev. | — |

**Verdict:** (d) ≫ (c) > (a) ≫ (b). A camera mount is the part where a mesh is most dangerous: it looks correct and screws together wrong.

### Part 4: **Flange adapter — DN50 to DN65, 4 × Ø18 bolt circle, 6 mm face, a 3 mm fillet at the hub**

| Approach | What happens | Why | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(a) Code-first** | A flange is a revolve + a pattern + a hole + a fillet. The revolve is parametric and good. The **fillet is where LLM CAD code breaks hardest**: BenchCAD per-op recall **fillet 0.0%** (gpt-5.3) and 12.0% (thinking); `chamfer` 8.6%. On top: OCCT fillet failures (build123d #1506: *"the most common thing people get stuck on"*), and CadQuery #2114 where a failed fillet **segfaults the process**. Expect the fillet missing, or a rounded blob. | BenchCAD Table 13; build123d #1506; CadQuery #2114. | 2605.10865, GitHub issues |
| **(b) Text-to-mesh** | The fillet is a **sub-voxel-radius** feature — precisely what Hi3D's SSR table shows failing. At 1536³ TRELLIS.2 slices at **17%** and Pixal3D at **7%**; Hi3D's own table shows SSR *falls* as resolution rises, i.e. **resolving finer geometry makes the part less printable**. A fillet on a mesh is also not a fillet: it's a curved band with no radius parameter, so you cannot change it. | Hi3D Technical Report Table 1 + §3 methodology; TRELLIS.2 §F (holes, aliasing); OmniMech (volume error 317.9%). | Hi3D TR, 2512.14692, 2608.05539 |
| **(c) GUI-driven** | A flange is a revolve — CADWorld's Part category (77 tasks) includes *"revolve"* explicitly. But the 4-hole pattern and the fillet are state-dependent operations that must be applied *after* the revolve, and CADWorld's F5 (`wrong_document_structure`) is the modal terminal-ablation failure. Also: CADWorld covers Part modeling but the tasks are *not* production flanges. | CADWorld taxonomy + terminal ablation. | 2609.16251 |
| **(d) Bridge** | Best. Zoo's KCL Standard Library has *patterns* and *holes*; Procedura's typed mates and machine-checkable commits suit a flanged adapter with a bolt circle; **mesh2cad explicitly handles bolt circles** and its scope list is *"plates, brackets, and boxes with holes, bolt circles, slots, bosses… countersunk and counterbored holes… and fillets"* — literally this part. Its six real-part benchmarks come back **exact** with ≤0.39% volume error. But mesh2cad also shows the failure mode: *"At 0.2 mm [noise] the bracket falls back on every seed… one seed keeps only three of the four rounds and the Hausdorff rises to 0.85 mm"* — **the fillet is the first thing to go.** | mesh2cad README + docs/limits.md + docs/real-parts.md; Zoo KCL stdlib. | GitHub, zoo.dev |

**Verdict:** (d) > (a) > (c) ≫ (b). Note that every approach's weakest link on this part is the *same*: the fillet.
