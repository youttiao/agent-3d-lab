## TASK A — Physical-world feedback / embodied verification for CAD

### A.1 Headline answer

**There is no 2026 arXiv paper in which an agent uses an actual 3D printer, robot arm, or other *physical* artifact as the primary feedback loop to verify and repair the CAD of a mechanical part.** What exists falls into four tiers, from closest to farthest:

| Tier | What the feedback actually is | Best example |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Real hardware, real feedback** | Physical robots built and filmed; agent redesigns after seeing real failure | **RoboBuilder** (arXiv:2611.23855, 2026-11-18) |
| **2. Rigid-body simulation of a mechanism** | Chrono + Bullet simulation of candidate-specific physical tests | **FunCAD-Harness / FunCADBench** (arXiv:2610.26861, 2026-10-19) |
| **3. Slicer/process-rule feedback** | 45° overhang detection, DFM rules, G-code | **AgentsCAD** (arXiv:2607.02448) |
| **4. Render/kernel feedback only** | Renders + OpenCASCADE measurements | CADSmith (2603.26512), Adam, Zoo Zookeeper |

The 3D-printer feedback that *does* exist is **print-process monitoring**, not CAD verification: `LLM-3D Print` (arXiv:2408.14307, 2024-08-26) and the BaratiLab skill library ship code that watches the print layer-by-layer and changes printer parameters — it never edits the CAD model.

### A.2 Tier 1 — RoboBuilder: the only 2026 paper with real physical verification in the design loop

**arXiv:2611.23855** — *RoboBuilder: Agentic Robot Co-Design with Real-world Video Feedback in the Loop* — https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23855

This is the strongest existing evidence for "physical-world feedback fixes the design." Verbatim from the abstract:

> "We introduce RoboBuilder, a benchmark in which coding agents co-design a robot's hardware and program from standard parts, motors and a custom 3D-printed part, and every design that passes in simulation is built and tested on a physical course. Simulation alone is not enough: under public part data, none of 15 initial passing designs crossed the real course, so an agent learns the simulator from measurements it requests and videos of real runs."

Measured facts (from the full text):
- **135 physical robots built by hand** from ~13,000 pieces, **≈240 hours** of assembly/repair/testing; **16,427 simulations of 12,774 distinct robots**; **461 paired real videos** (all released).
- The custom part is written by the agent **in CadQuery** and 3D-printed: *"one custom part type that the agent writes in CadQuery"*, up to four copies.
- **Reality gap:** *"Of the designs that pass in the learned simulator, 66% cross the real course: 77% at level 1, 68% at level 2 and 42% at level 3."*
- **Closing the loop works:** *"One round of the loop removes 72% of these failures (from 18 to 5), and the share of simulated passes that cross for real rises from 73% to 93%."*
- Task pass rates: **98% at level 1, 70% at level 2, 48% at level 3.** Search baselines over the full inventory: *"Search over the full inventory passes no task."* A single LLM call with no validator: *"A single LLM call, with no validator or simulator feedback, passes at most two tasks."*
- The printed part is what rescues hard cases: *"The printed part rescues nine failed tasks, mostly at level 3."*
- Agents also fix their simulator from video: *"Opus 5.5 watched its spoked wheels spin against a bump ridge and lowered the friction of hard plastic on the bump rods from 0.39 to 0.30."*
- Authors' own caveat: *"The simulator is fitted from limited observations, a fixed camera and bench tests without force or contact data, so some parameters are not identifiable."*

**Important framing for the report:** RoboBuilder is a *robot* benchmark, not a mechanical-part benchmark. Its CAD artifact is one bespoke part per robot, not a plate/bracket family.

### A.3 Tier 2 — FunCAD-Harness: rigid-body simulation of a CAD *mechanism* (the CAD-bench-adjacent item)

**arXiv:2610.26861** — *Can It Actually Work? Synthesizing Functional CAD Mechanisms* — https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26861

This is the closest thing to "LLM agent writes parametric CAD and a simulator checks fit/assembly/interference."

- **FunCADBench: 46 mechanism families** — *"clamps, transmissions, mechanical counters, alarm clocks, and coin-sorting devices"*, with *"interdependent and multi-step operations."*
- Simulation stack: *"Our simulation environment uses **Project Chrono** for rigid-body dynamics and **Bullet** for collision detection."* Gear couplings may use a declared equivalent model instead of resolving tooth contacts.
- Success = the assembly *"passes all required functional checks and all applicable source-geometry checks (e.g., collision checks)."*
- **Headline numbers (Table 2,46 tasks, independent final evaluation):**

| Setting | Astra | Grok |
|---|---|---|
| Requirements only (agent picks own verification) | **1/46** | 3/46 |
| Simulation tools | 8/46 | 10/46 |
| Binary pass/fail feedback (no diagnostics) | 0/46 | 8/46 |
| Full harness, **constructor vision removed** | 20/46 | 18/46 |
| **Full harness** | **22/46** | 19/46 |

- **The finding that matters most for a "physical feedback" argument:** removing *diagnostic* feedback collapses Astra from 22 tasks to **zero**; and *"Agents obtain geometric and physical information primarily through CAD programs and simulation, with little use of rendered images."* Removing visual access entirely cost only 2 tasks (22→20).
- Concrete interference failures are visible in renders, e.g. *"The bicycle's steering assembly intersects the fixed frame (Task 7, requirements-only)"* and *"the clock's hour wheel overlaps the alarm lever despite separate escapement and striker tests."*
- **Explicitly not a printing loop:** *"Because fabricating and assembling each candidate is costly, physical simulation offers a practical way to examine these interactions."*

### A.4 The "CAD-bench" ICML 2026 workshop benchmark (the one your brief asked about)

Your brief was right that this exists, but it is **not on arXiv** — it is an ICML 2026 workshop **poster**.

- **Poster page:** https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/67851
- **OpenReview:** https://openreview.net/forum?id=eG1jFVEHht (I hit a Cloudflare challenge page; the ICML abstract below is the verbatim source)
- **Title:** *CAD-bench: Benchmarking Language Models on Functional CAD Generation* — Dhruv Saini — Poster, *The Second Workshop on Agents in the Wild: Safety, Security, and Beyond*, ICML 2026
- **Cited as reference [20] in** arXiv:2611.02659 (Parametric CAD Bench v3): https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02659

**Verbatim abstract (from https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/67851):**

> "Language-model agents are increasingly able to operate computer-aided design (CAD) toolchains, but current evaluations often measure only executable code, rendered appearance, or coarse geometric similarity. These proxies can miss the properties that matter for reliable artifact-producing agents in engineering settings: exact dimensions, mating interfaces, standards-like details, and functional behavior of assemblies. We introduce CAD-bench, an execution-based benchmark for evaluating language-model CAD agents. **CAD-bench contains 17 tasks across four difficulty tiers, ranging from basic solids to threaded mating pairs and functional gear trains.** Each submission is executed, exported as geometry, and evaluated with task-specific checks, including **dimensional and pose verification, reference-geometry gates, thread-profile analysis, and Blender-based rigid-body simulation.** The benchmark supports both one-shot CAD-code generation and agent harnesses that produce final STEP artifacts in an executable environment. **Initial results show that CAD-bench is not saturated by current systems. The strongest standalone model reaches 59.9% overall, while functional tasks remain near zero for most standalone runs.** Agent harnesses perform better than one-shot generation, but still fail frequently on interfaces, standards-like details, and mechanisms. CAD-bench therefore exposes a gap between producing runnable CAD artifacts and producing CAD artifacts that satisfy mechanically meaningful task requirements…"

**Answering your specific sub-questions:**
- **17 tasks: CONFIRMED.**
- **Four difficulty tiers: CONFIRMED** — "basic solids to threaded mating pairs and gear trains."
- **Rigid-body simulation in Blender: CONFIRMED** — "Blender-based rigid-body simulation."
- **Per-task breakdown: NOT PUBLICLY AVAILABLE.** The poster PDF is behind an ICML login (`https://icml.cc/virtual/2026/poster/60898`) and the OpenReview page is behind a browser challenge. I could not extract the task list, tier names, or per-tier scores.
- **Critically: CAD-bench is a *different benchmark* from Parametric CAD Bench v3** despite the similar name. Parametric CAD Bench v3 says so itself: *"Despite the similar name, it is a separate benchmark with a different evaluation target."*

### A.7 Render-and-measure feedback — VERIFIED, with the key quotes

**BenchCAD 2.0** — https://benchcad.com/blog/benchcad-2.html (BLOG · 2026-10-12; citation: `{benchcad2_2026, title = {BenchCAD 2.0: Benchmarking Programmatic CAD on Real Engineering Work}, author = {{BenchCAD Team}}, year = {2026}, howpublished = {\url{https://benchcad.com/blog/benchcad-2.html}}}`)

Your brief's characterization is **partly right and partly wrong**. BenchCAD 2.0 is *not* a rigid-body-simulation benchmark, and it does **not** ship a render tool. Verbatim:

> "We ran five frontier models on Core at every reasoning-effort setting, each with up to 30 rounds per case in an offline sandbox that has **Python's standard library, CadQuery, NumPy, SciPy and an image-cropping tool, but no built-in rendering tool; models can write and run their own rendering code.**"

> "At low effort, GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.5 usually answer parts from views in a round or two, **rarely rendering to check** (our post on 2D proxies). They score 0.14 and 0.06 at low effort, against 0.59 and 0.57 at max."

> "In the trajectories, the model at low and medium effort treats running its program as handing it in. **It waits for a rendered check that the task does not give**, then repeats the same program until the rounds run out."

> "The assembly and board answers look right at a glance: the frame has every part, the board nearly every component. What goes wrong is what a glance misses: which way a part faces, which pins share a net. **Better CAD agents need checks on placement and connectivity, not just better generation.**"

**Geometry engine underneath — answer: Zoo's OWN proprietary kernel, written in Rust, running on Zoo's remote servers. NOT Onshape, NOT build123d, NOT FreeCAD, NOT OpenCASCADE.**

Verbatim from https://zoo.dev/docs/developer-tools/engine-api:
> "The Engine API is a web-based programming interface for software developers to interact with **a new CAD kernel on powerful remote computers**."

Verbatim from https://zoo.dev/docs/developer-tools/api:
> "This specification is produced by our **Rust source code** as a central source of truth."

Verbatim from https://zoo.dev/docs/developer-tools/engine-api:
> "Zoo offers all its CAD kernel instances on the best remote compute available."

**KCL = KittyCAD Language.** Verbatim from https://zoo.dev/docs/kcl:
> "KCL is KittyCAD's programming language for CAD. It is **the source of truth behind Zoo models**, so geometry created in Zoo Design Studio can be generated as KCL, edited as KCL, and shared as plain text in `.kcl` files."

**Does it emit STEP / B-Rep?**
- **B-Rep: yes.** From the Text-to-CAD launch (https://zoo.dev/blog/introducing-text-to-cad): *"There are lots of text-to-3d models that exist for gaming asset use cases but there are no Text-to-CAD models. The distinction being that **we do not use point clouds, we generate B-Rep surfaces. So you can import a STEP file from Text-to-CAD into any existing CAD program and edit it.** The existing text-to-3d models generate meshes so if you were to import that into an existing CAD program, it would just be one large amorphous blob and not editable in any useful way."*
- **STEP: yes.** https://zoo.dev/docs/developer-tools/file-format-api: *"A simple HTTP request to `/file/conversion/{input_format}/{output_format}` is enough to convert between CAD file formats."* CLI: `zoo file convert`, `zoo kcl export`.
- Artifact formats from Text-to-CAD: *"The artifacts that come from the initial API call are a STEP file and a glTF file."*

**Documented limitations** (verbatim from https://zoo.dev/docs/kcl-lang/known-issues):
> "**Import**: Right now you can import a file, but you cannot edit it — even if that file has BREP data. Imports have type `ImportedGeometry` rather than `Solid`, so functions that operate on Solid will reject imported geometry. Recreate the geometry natively in KCL if you need to modify it."
>
> "**CSG Booleans**: Coplanar (bodies that share a plane) unions, subtractions, and intersections are not currently supported."
>
> "**Chamfers on non-90° edges have incorrect lengths**: Chamfers on edges with a dihedral angle other than 90° between their faces (or with variable dihedral angle) didn't respect the provided length, instead cutting back further than the requested length. This is fixed in KCL 3.0."

And from https://zoo.dev/research/introducing-kcl (limitations section):
> "One limitation KCL has is that **it does not properly support declaratively positioning 3D parts relative to each other**. That's something we need to improve, because manually positioning parts for assemblies is a waste of valuable mechanical engineering time. We're actively working to extend KCL with a 3D constraint solver that works similarly to existing CAD software, allowing users to, say, constraint a bolt to be concentric with the hole it fits into…"

**Is it open source?**
- Zoo calls KCL *"Zoo's **open-source programming language** for CAD modeling"* (https://zoo.dev/blog/announcing-zookeeper) and the docs say *"All Zoo docs are open source"* (https://zoo.dev/docs).

### B.6 The Text2CAD family — and a correction to your brief

> ⚠️ **The arXiv ID in the brief is wrong.** `2412.04253` is **"Hilbert's 10th Problem via Mordell curves"** — a number-theory paper. I verified this by direct fetch of https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04253.

- *"our model significantly outperforms the baseline DeepCAD in terms of **median CD and invalidity ratio by a factor of ∼88.7 and ∼10.75** respectively."*

- GPT-4V preference: *"Text2CAD outperforms DeepCAD at the intermediate (L2) and expert (L3) levels, leading by as much as **18.6% and 27.18%** respectively"*; *"the performance gap … is minimal at the abstract (L0) and beginner (L1) levels, despite Text2CAD losing by 2.8% at the beginner level."*

As reported in Table 1, this occurs in approximately **1%** of the test samples."* Failure causes: *"the model predicts invalid sketch or extrusion parameters, such as the same start and end points for lines or arcs, or zero values for extrusion depth on both sides."*

**Stated limitations (verbatim, §6):**
> "Firstly, LLaVA-NeXT is sensitive to the perspective distortions in multi-view images, which affects the generated shape descriptions and final LLM-generated prompts. **On "ambiguity" — honest finding:** Text2CAD itself does **not** use the word "ambiguity". **Why Text2CAD matters for the "functional mechanical part" argument — and where it does *not*.**
Its contribution is *designer-legible sequential construction at multiple abstraction levels*, which is what makes a model **editable** rather than a blob: the paper explicitly contrasts itself with KittyCAD's modeling app ("the generated CAD models are obtained in the form of solid-body, and not decomposed to its intermediate sketch-and-extrusion steps") and with LLM-written FreeCAD/OpenSCAD scripts ("*will not be designer-friendly, not suitable for beginner-level designers, will not automate the development process in easy ways, and will restrict the re-usability of the scripts in case of complex shapes*"). **But**: (a) the output is a sketch-extrude *sequence*, **not** STEP/B-rep, and (b) its training distribution is rectangular/cylindrical DeepCAD geometry, so it is the wrong tool for bolted joints, brackets and plates with holes. 2026 evidence that this limitation is real: Text2CAD-Bench (below) shows the trained model has **IR 2% but CD 220 and near-zero IoU** — i.e. *"it consistently generates executable code without capturing geometric specifications."*

## TASK C — Mesh→CAD repair round trip

### C.1 Why convert a generated mesh back to CAD (primary argument)

From arXiv:2608.00799 (CADENA), §Introduction, verbatim:
> "Editable parametric CAD models are the working currency of mechanical engineering, yet most existing 3D assets — scanned parts, legacy archives, models exported without history — exist only as raw geometry. **A mesh can be rendered and printed but not changed: it has no notion of a bore, a fillet or a wall, only triangles, so there is no handle by which to widen a hole or lengthen a bracket.** A part is almost never reused exactly as found — it must be adapted to the standards of the product it goes into — and every such adaptation is an edit to a parameter that only a program exposes. The case is stronger still for scanned input, whose surfaces are noisy and edges rounded: **fitting geometry to such a measurement reproduces its defects, whereas recovering a program reconstructs what the part is** — a cylinder of some diameter, a hole at some position — and with it idealised, manufacturable geometry."

**Where it *doesn't* work — verbatim from docs/limits.md and docs/real-parts.md:**
- **Fillets degrade first.** *"At 0.2 mm [noise] the bracket falls back on every seed. The plate is exact on 2 of 3 seeds, and one of those solids is missing a corner. The block is a valid solid on every seed, but one seed keeps only three of the four rounds and the Hausdorff rises to 0.85 mm."*
- **Countersinks are noise-sensitive.** *"Countersinks and narrow chamfers are exact through about 0.05 mm of noise; a 2 mm chamfer and a wide drafted wall stay exact at 0.2 mm. The countersunk plate's 90° countersink … at 0.2 mm noise is exact on one of three seeds. On the other two the conical band and the hole wall meet at a single vertex, so those seeds fall back to a valid faceted solid."*
- **Spheres rarely come back as spheres.** *"A sphere that runs into an edge or a cylinder in any other way falls back to the hybrid solid."* *"A cone that ends in its apex falls back to the hybrid solid."*
- **Split circular features get sewn.** y-belt-holder: *"One of those circles is split across four coplanar pads that do not touch, and is sewn as four arcs."*
- **Boolean silhouettes can blow up.** y-motor-holder: *"The intersection of the full-length silhouettes is 7.924 mm off (30.07% volume), over the limit of 0.266 mm, so the script is a union of partial-depth extrusions instead."* y-rod-holder was **refused** at an earlier version (*"the refusal was 0.745 mm"*) before being rebuilt from a raster.
- **Belt-profile patches stay faceted.** *"Those B-spline patches are still planes there"* — the residual 0.16–0.22 mm Hausdorff.
- And the overall honesty statement: *"It does not recover organic or sculpted shapes, scans, or free-form surfaces. When a fit is poor it does not invent a surface."*

**Verdict for your report:** mesh→CAD round trip **demonstrably works** for the specific mechanical-part families you named — plates, brackets, boxes with holes, bolt circles, countersunk/counterbored holes, and simple fillets — when the source mesh came from a CAD tool (long, clean triangles) rather than a noisy scan. It fails on fillets/countersinks above ~0.05–0.2 mm of noise, on spheres, on freeform/ribbed outlines, and on features split across coplanar pads.

**Key framing statements (verbatim):**
- *"Moving from DeepCAD to CADENA-Bench costs every learned method roughly half its GMS: cadrille 94.8 → 49.8, CAD-Recode 92.9 → 48.9, CADEvolve 95.3 → 52.9, CADReasoner 94.9 → 52.9. CADENA-RL falls from 97.0 to 67.0, so its margin over the strongest baseline widens from 1.7 points on DeepCAD to 12.2 on CADENA-Bench. **Test sets built from sketch–extrude corpora understate the difficulty of the task they are taken to measure.**"*
- *"CADFit is the one baseline that does not learn a generator… It is the weakest method on DeepCAD (89.8 GMS) yet the strongest baseline on MCB (60.1) and CADENA-Bench (54.8), because fitting geometry directly does not depend on the training distribution matching the test parts."* Its IR is 25.6–29.7% though, and *"its means are computed over an easier-than-average subset and should be read as optimistic."*

**"Characteristic failures" (Appendix A.7, Fig. 5) — verbatim:**
> "**Five parts on which CADENA fails**, with the target above and the returned reconstruction below. They illustrate three of the four modes discussed in this section: **countable features are approximated rather than counted**, so the impeller and the radial fan keep the right body and the wrong blades; **helical geometry outside the DSL's vocabulary is replaced by the nearest available primitive**, a thread standing in for a coil spring; and **where no single canonical view resolves the shape, the reconstruction collapses to a rough envelope of it, as in the valve body and the bracket.** None of these is a failure to emit valid CadQuery — every reconstruction shown builds and is watertight, and would be counted in the means of Table 1."

The four modes, verbatim:
1. *"Countable features are approximated rather than counted. On gears and splined shafts the reconstruction reproduces the body and the general character of the teeth while getting their number wrong. The residual after the body is built is a thin, repetitive shell that changes little whether the count is right or off by one, so the signal that would correct the count is weak exactly where it is needed. This is the largest contributor to the gap on gears & bearings, our weakest family on CADENA-Bench."*
2. *"Occluded geometry is invisible to the observation. The policy sees eight views — six axis-aligned and two isometric. Internal cavities, blind bores and undercuts that project identically to a solid region are not represented in the observation at all, so no amount of stepping recovers them — the loop converges happily to a solid whose exterior matches."*
3. *"Out-of-vocabulary features are approximated by what the DSL has. Geometry that no operation in Table 6 can express — freeform blends, non-circular helical profiles, draft — is approximated by stacks of extrusions. The result is often close in Chamfer distance and clearly wrong to an engineer, which is precisely the discrepancy GMS is designed to expose."*
4. *"Early frame errors are not recovered. When the first operation fixes a plane or an origin inconsistent with the target, later steps can add material that reduces the residual locally without ever undoing the original error."*

### C.4 CADFather — arXiv:2610.09127 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09127)

Method: *"an autonomous agentic system that coordinates complementary tools to recover parametric CAD programs from 3D meshes. A vision-language assistant inspects renders of the target and intermediate reconstructions, then decides which candidate CAD programs to extend, which tools to invoke, how many proposals to generate, and when to finish."* Tools = learned stepwise generator (CADENA-RL checkpoint, bfloat16) + deterministic geometric proposals + numerical parameter optimizer. Assistant = **Qwen3.8-27B (FP8)**. Budgets: 24 assistant steps, ≤12 ops, 1000 s wall time, 80 generator samples, 6 deterministic-proposal calls, 10 optimizer calls, 120 executions.

**Main results (full test sets 8046 / 1725 / 5000 parts):** DeepCAD IoU **0.987** (vs CADENA-RL sampling 0.966), Fusion360 **0.976** (vs 0.952), MCB **0.913** (vs 0.895). GMS 0.983 / 0.960 / 0.766. **IR = 0 on all three.**

**Cost analysis (verbatim):**
> "CADFather reaches an IoU of 0.943 after four assistant steps at **$0.016 per part**, and 0.968 at its full budget of **$0.046 per part**. For comparison, **Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra with tools report 0.962 at $5.94 per part and 0.959 at $1.75 per part**, respectively."

On BenchCAD: *"CADFather reaches a voxel IoU of 0.968 with no invalid predictions, against 0.910 for CADENA-RL… and at most 0.752 for image-conditioned systems without tools."* CADENA-Bench is the most expensive set: *"13.0 steps and 307 s per part, with only 25.0% of parts ended by the assistant."*

**Ablation (what actually matters):** *"removing deterministic proposals costs the most: it lowers the mean score by 0.012–0.110 … and raises IR from zero to 0.007–0.124, whereas removing the optimizer lowers the mean score by only 0.004–0.006 and leaves IR at zero."* And: *"On MCB, the optimizer adds almost nothing on top of stepwise generation."*

**Failure analysis (Fig. 7, verbatim):**
> "Left: high IoU with low GMS, where the volume is matched but the **knurl** (MCB) and the **shallow pockets** (Fusion360) are missing. Right: parts stopped by the wall-time limit (MCB, **a spring reconstructed as a tube**) and by the execution limit (Fusion360, **a bracket with incomplete ribs**)."

**Stated limitations:** *"Our evaluation measures geometric reconstruction and program validity, not recovery of the original design history, manufacturing intent, or engineering constraints. It covers individual parts rather than assemblies, tolerances, or simulation-validated designs."*

### C.5 CADMAGEDDON — arXiv:2611.22563 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22563)

*"an open-source agent that reconstructs editable CAD from meshes and can run locally on single-GPU systems and current-generation laptops."* Model: **Qwen3.8-27B**, FP8, *"fits on a single 48 GB GPU."* Code: github.com/kulibinai/cadmageddon (14★). Method: *"The method decomposes this complex task into a chain of simple visual decisions about what to construct and what to correct. Geometric tools support precise construction, allowing the agent to focus on visual reasoning."* *"a vision–language model chooses only the structure of each feature, and fitting supplies its values."* Writes **both CadQuery and native FreeCAD** programs.

**Headline numbers (Table 1; subsets: DeepCAD/Fusion360/MCB 300 each, BenchCAD 1000, CADENA-Bench 60):**
- *"It reaches the highest IoU and GMS and the lowest CD on all five benchmarks."*
- *"**IoU rises from 0.862 to 0.933 on MCB and from 0.918 to 0.981 on CADENA-Bench.** The largest gains are in GMS, the metric that compares surface types: **from 0.705 to 0.818 on MCB and from 0.800 to 0.909 on CADENA-Bench.** CD on MCB falls from 0.260 to 0.196."*
- IR ≤ 0.67% (vs CADFit 18.7–24.3%).
- BenchCAD (Table 2): *"CADMAGEDDON, driven by the mid-sized Qwen3.8-27B and scored with the same 64³ voxel IoU, reaches **0.991 on a 1000-part subset with 0.3% invalid predictions**."* Frontier models with images + a Python sandbox self-report up to 0.963 on 1000-part subsets; without a sandbox at most 0.747.
- BenchCAD-2 task 3 (23 parts): *"With the mesh as input, CADMAGEDDON reaches **0.933** with CadQuery at **$1.05 per part**."* Frontier models on the public leaderboard: *"none exceeds a part score of 0.59."* The same Qwen3.8-27B in a general-purpose agent harness (Qwen Code) reaches only 0.790 with 2/23 parts missing.
- *"On gears, sprockets, the bevel gear and the impeller, the baselines reproduce the overall disc, cone or hub but **merge or omit the teeth and blades**, while CADMAGEDDON recovers them."*

**Important limitation for your report (verbatim):**
> "The emitted CadQuery **can be edited, but its dimensions are literal values rather than named parameters**, and **fillets and chamfers are explicit solids rather than edge operations**; **recovering design intent is beyond the current system.**"

So: CADMAGEDDON gets *geometric* reconstruction right but does **not** deliver the named-parameter parametric model that makes a part re-editable in an engineering sense. That's the gap between mesh→CAD-as-geometry and mesh→CAD-as-design-intent.

## Honest gaps and cautions

1. **No physical-printer-in-the-loop CAD repair paper exists on arXiv as of 2026-11-23.** The two nearest are LLM-3D Print (2024, controls the printer) and the BaratiLab `llm-3d-print-monitor` skill (camera per layer, in-print corrections). If you want to claim "an agent verified CAD by printing it," you must either cite RoboBuilder (real robots, CadQuery part printed) or state that this does not yet exist.
2. **CAD-bench's per-task list is not public.** Only the abstract (ICML) — poster behind login, OpenReview behind a Cloudflare challenge.
3. **KCL's main repository is unlocatable right now** (`KCL-Language/kcl` → 404; `KCL-Language` org → 404). Do not quote a KCL star count.
4. **Zoo's CAD kernel is closed and hosted.** Only the *language* (KCL) and the *docs* are open. Everything geometric is a paid API on Zoo's Rust server-side kernel. This is materially different from text-to-cad (build123d/OpenCASCADE) which is fully local and MIT.
5. **Two arXiv IDs in the brief are wrong:** Text2CAD is **2409.17106** (not 2412.04253); and the **CADENA-Bench family names** in the brief don't match the paper. Verified the others: CADFather 2610.09127 ✓, CADENA 2608.00799 ✓, CADMAGEDDON 2611.22563 ✓, CAD-Recode 2412.14042 ✓.
6. **BuildCAD AI and Adam make unmeasured claims.** Neither publishes a benchmark; both are vendor pages. Procedura's MechBench-36 is the only place "Adam CAD" is evaluated by a third party (0.799 vs Procedura 0.828).
7. **WebSearch was unavailable for this turn**, so I could not do general-web triangulation (e.g. for CAD-Robot, MechBench++, FabBench, PrintBench). Those four are reported as **COULD NOT VERIFY** on arXiv + the Zoo/BenchCAD/GitHub surfaces I could reach — not as "does not exist."
8. **Zenith-level caveat for the round-trip argument:** the *deterministic* path (mesh2cad) is dramatically more reliable on real mechanical parts than any *learned* path — mesh2cad gets 6/6 Prusa parts exact; the best learned system (CADFather) still gets GMS 0.66 on gears/bearings and 0.67 on springs/fasteners, and CADMAGEDDON still cannot produce named parameters. Any2026 report should present "mesh → editable parametric mechanical CAD" as a *solved-for-simple-primitives, unsolved-for-real-parts* problem.
